# Welcome to Sun Style Internal Martial Arts!

### My Journey in Martial Arts
I was born and raised in South Korea and am now in my mid-40s. I’ve loved Chinese martial arts since childhood, especially Tai Chi. In my early 30s, I spent four years studying Tai Chi seriously, putting in plenty of time and savings, and even traveling to Wudang Mountain in China for a seminar.

Still, something felt missing. I wanted to understand how the movements and Push Hands actually worked when someone pushed back, but most classes focused strictly on slow, gentle health exercises.

### Finding Real Practice in Korea
In 2016, I joined a <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xingyiquan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Xingyiquan</a> group in Seoul. My teacher trained in Sun-style Xingyiquan, Baguazhang, and Tai Chi. From day one, his rule was simple: a movement makes no sense if you don't know what it does against a resisting partner.

I loved every session. Our school places heavy weight on hands-on posture checks and live sparring. Without a teacher physically correcting your alignment, it’s almost impossible to get internal structure right on your own.

### 🤝 Why I Am Starting This in Edmonton
After returning to Canada in 2024, I missed having people to train with. Practicing solo in the backyard helps your basics, but you can’t truly test your structure without another person pushing, pulling, and checking your balance.

In sports, great coaches are rarely the star players:
- In hockey, Jon Cooper never played a game in the NHL, but coached Tampa Bay to two Stanley Cups.
- In soccer, José Mourinho had a quiet playing career before winning championships across Europe.
- In baseball, Buck Showalter never made the majors as a player, yet won Manager of the Year four times.

Natural athletes often move on instinct. They just do it, and they usually struggle to explain how. To be completely honest, I was never a natural athlete. I was stiff, uncoordinated, and struggled with the simplest alignments. Qualifying to guide others typically takes three to four years, but it took me nearly eight years of steady practice under my teacher in Korea to earn my certificate and permission to teach.

I’m looking for training partners to practice and grow with. My teacher’s school in Korea was built on three straightforward pillars, and that is exactly what we focus on:

- 🌿 **Health:** Standing meditation (Zhan Zhuang) and relaxed rooting that realign your posture and take pressure off your lower back and joints.
- 🎯 **Fun:** The hands-on enjoyment of partner drills—feeling how force transfers, testing short-range power (Fajin), and learning through safe, cooperative push hands.
- 🛡️ **Self-Defense:** Practical body mechanics, joint controls, and direct strikes that work through structure and timing rather than brute muscular force.

### Who Would Enjoy This
- **Tai Chi practitioners curious about the martial side:** If you know the solo forms but wonder what the movements are actually doing, this is for you. We explore joint locks (Qin-na), trips, sweeps, and how to stay grounded when someone actually pushes you.
- **Beginners wanting solid body mechanics:** No prior martial arts background is needed. We don't do movie gymnastics or talk about mystical energy. We focus on practical body mechanics: alignment, balance, and using your whole body weight efficiently.

### What to Expect
- **Core focus on Xingyiquan:** While Sun style blends Xingyi, Bagua, and Tai Chi, our foundation starts with Xingyiquan. It is the most direct of the three arts for learning solid structure and short-range power.
- **Drills over long routines:** Rather than memorizing 50-step forms, we spend our time on standing postures, partner push drills, and testing whether our balance holds under pressure.
- **Safe, collaborative pace:** We train carefully so nobody gets hurt. The goal is to challenge each other's structure, give honest feedback, and improve together.
