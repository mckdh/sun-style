---
title: "What Does “Application” Really Mean in Xingyiquan?"
---
In my introduction, I pointed out a common frustration with how traditional Chinese martial arts are often taught today. 

Many Tai Chi schools focus almost entirely on slow, graceful movements and health benefits. There is nothing wrong with health or moving gracefully. But too often, students practice routines for years without ever learning what those movements were actually designed to do in combat.

A fair question to ask me would be:

> **"If you say applications matter so much, how much do you actually know about Xingyiquan applications, and what exactly do you teach?"**

To answer that honestly, we have to look at what "application" really means. It breaks down into two main layers.

---

## 1. The Visible Layer: What Does This Movement Do?

Xingyiquan is built on the **Five Elements** (五行拳: *Pi, Beng, Zuan, Pao, Heng*) and the **Twelve Animal Forms** (十二形). 

If you simply watch someone perform these forms, their combat purpose is almost impossible to guess. 

Take the **Dragon Form** (龍形, *Longxing*), for example. At one point, you twist and drop your body low toward the ground. To anyone watching, it looks like an odd stretch or an awkward dance posture. You would never guess how it works in a real fight.
![[PARA/2.Area/Sun Style/1.Literature/Contents/attachments/main-fun-image.png|381]]
[The Dragon Form]

Behind that posture lies a direct and brutal intent. One application I teach for Dragon Form is straightforward: you grab and pull the opponent’s arm to yank them off-balance, while simultaneously stomping down right above their knee. If executed with real force, it can easily break their leg.

Once you know that, the movement stops looking like a strange dance. It has a clear target and a combat purpose.

This is true across Taijiquan, Baguazhang, and Xingyiquan. You cannot guess the application just by looking at the outer form. Traditionally, this practical knowledge had to be handed down directly from master to student. Because old masters guarded their secrets closely and only taught trusted disciples, many practitioners only inherited empty routines (*Taolu*, 套路). The outer shells survived, but the practical combat knowledge was lost.

---

## 2. The Deeper Layer: What Makes It Work?

Knowing how to hit, push, or break a joint is still only the first step. 

That is just knowing *what* the movement is. The real question is:

> **What makes the movement actually work against resistance?**

This is where Xingyiquan becomes truly fascinating. The real heart of our art is developing what internal martial arts call **Neijin** (內勁, internal power).

The word "internal" often confuses people:
* **The Fantasy View:** Some people hear "internal power" and imagine shooting energy blasts (*Qi*) or throwing opponents across the room without touching them.
* **The Skeptical View:** Others assume the whole thing is just a fake martial-arts scam or movie myth.

![[Pasted image 20261003160554.png|260]]
(No, internal power is not shooting Hadouken fireballs like in video games—nor is it a fake scam.)

Both views completely miss the point.

Real Neijin is completely grounded in **body mechanics and physics**. It comes from:
* Proper skeletal alignment and structure
* Deep relaxation (*Song*, 放鬆) instead of stiff muscular tension
* Whole-body connection, where the feet, waist, spine, and hands move as a single unit
* Efficient force transfer from the ground through the body

You are not merely memorizing canned fight combinations like *"If he does A, I do B."* You are training your body to generate, transfer, and absorb force in a completely different way. 

Learning a strike is easy. Changing how your body creates and handles force is the real discipline.

---

## I Am Still Learning Too

I want to be transparent: **I am still actively training and developing my own Neijin.**

When I compare myself to my master, I clearly feel the gap in depth and refinement. I am not presenting myself as some enlightened kung fu grandmaster who has unlocked all the secrets. I am an active student of the art. I train every day, and I still discover weaknesses in my own body.

What I *can* offer is the clear, systematic training method handed down through our lineage. We don't overwhelm beginners with deep theory on day one. First, we fix your alignment and build correct structure. Once your body is ready, the deeper layers naturally open up.

---

## Summary: What Application Really Means

When I teach Xingyiquan applications, we train two things:

1. **The Technique:** What the movement actually does to an opponent (strikes, takedowns, locks).
2. **The Engine:** How your body mechanics and internal power (Neijin) make that technique functional.

Knowing what a movement does is exciting. But training your body so that the movement actually works is where true martial arts begin.
