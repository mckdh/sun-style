# 🥋 Training Locations & Spaces

Most martial arts schools train indoors. But at the Korea Xingyiquan Research Association, my master taught us to train outdoors as much as possible.

---

## 🌿 Training Outdoors: A Real Tradition

During my 8 years of training in Seoul with the **Korea Xingyiquan Research Association**, my master always taught us outdoors when the weather allowed.

Our main spot was the outer walkway of **Seoul World Cup Stadium**. It had a large roof overhead, so we could train outside in fresh air even when it rained or snowed.

![[Pasted image 20260911235235.png]]
*(Training at Seoul World Cup Stadium)*

When it rained too hard or the stadium had big events, we moved inside to my master's office.

![[Pasted image 20260911235150.png]]
*(Indoor training at my master's office)*

Here in Edmonton, I use the same approach with both outdoor and indoor spaces.

---

## 📍 1. Main Outdoor Spot: Amble Park (Ambleside)

![[Pasted image 20260911235506.png]]
*(Amble Park, the two toddlers are my twins. 👶👶)*

* **What it is:** A large open park with fresh air and green grass.
* **Why train here:** Xingyi forms are best practiced in a wide open space so you do not feel cramped, and sparring drills involve moving back and forth a lot, so having plenty of room is great.
* **Best for:** Spring, summer, and fall classes on dry days.

## 🏡 2. Second Outdoor Spot: Private Backyard

![[KakaoTalk_20260911_162613157 1.jpg]]

## 🏠 3. Indoor Space: Home Basement Studio

When the weather is bad or cold, we train in my home basement. It is an unfinished basement and not fancy, but it is a proper space for a few people to train. We have wall mats to practice power release (Fajin / 發勁), and we can practice takedowns (Shuai / 摔) and breakfalls that are hard to do outdoors.

---
